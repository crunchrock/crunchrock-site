the game

What is this

Bad Shrooms is a split-screen party FPS set in a cramped one-bedroom apartment. You and one friend ate mushrooms that were not, in fact, fine. An alien named Zlorp unfolded out of the living-room air, moved in, and started running the night like a game show — minigames built from whatever's lying around the apartment.

The screen is split down the middle, so nobody can lie about what they just did to you. Online is 1v1 and full-screen — different bit, same housemate.

We're a small studio polishing every minigame until it's exactly the right amount of unfair. Early Access, honestly: three minigames are playable start to finish tonight, and the rest are still cooking.

how you win

The jar

There is no points counter. Zlorp harvests a substance called loosh, and every round you win pours some into one jar you both share. It holds exactly a litre.

So you're not racing each other to a number. You're filling the same container, together, at different speeds. When it brims, the night ends — and whoever poured more walks into the finale alone.

The other thing the jar does: the fuller it gets, the more the game warps. Colour bends, the room breathes, the edges start disagreeing with you. By the last round you are winning a game that has become harder to look at. That's on purpose. You did that.

between rounds

The apartment

There's no lobby menu. Between minigames you're loose in the apartment, and the apartment is not ready for you.

  • Everything has physics. Grab it, throw it, wear it, regret it.
  • The Japanese toilet has a control panel. One of the buttons is a bidet. It is not a joke about a bidet — it is a functioning launcher, and your friend knows where you're standing.
  • Zlorp runs a shop. He sells joke guns and skins, and he never lets you forget what you paid for them.
  • Sixty characters. Ten are yours immediately. The other fifty sit there in the grid, visible, not yours.
  • A party is 2 to 15 rounds, your call before anyone commits. The jar is always a litre — you just decide how big the pours are.
